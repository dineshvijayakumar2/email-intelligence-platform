## What this is

For each customer, one actionable outreach: **what to pitch · to whom · with what context · at what timing** — each with a quantified confidence, and each backed by evidence you can check.

The engine works in two halves. **What to pitch** comes from cross-customer purchase patterns (market basket): a product this customer doesn't buy that customers with a similar basket reliably do. **Who, when, and the context** come from their own order history and email engagement. Every pitch is verified as a genuine zero-order gap before it appears here.

**How to read a card:** the headline is the action. The *Evidence* fold underneath shows the working — the purchase pattern (confidence, support, lift), the contact's track record, and the timing math — so any recommendation can be traced to the data.

## How to read confidence and lift

- **Confidence** = of customers who buy the predictor product, what % also buy the pitched product. Higher = more reliable pattern.
- **Lift** = how much *more* likely than the base rate. Lift 2.0 = twice as likely as average — a real, targeted signal. **Lift near 1.0 = the product is just popular generally** (not targeted), so high-confidence/low-lift pitches are flagged, not featured.

The strongest cards have **both** high confidence and high lift. They lead.

## Honest notes

- Confidence is the real rule % (P(Y|X)), never rounded up or relabelled.
- Every pitch verified as a genuine 0-order gap at company grain under qb-OR-classifier (back.pitch_evidence.gap_confirmation).
- No archetype / no AM comparison / no scorecard in any front or back field.
- Deferred QB-conflict companies flagged (conflict_flag + data_quality); unresolved ones sunk in ranking.
- Some accounts (mailboxes pending re-authentication) have **timing/recency that may be stale** — flagged per-card. A stale-looking account may be a sync gap, not silence.
- Pitches cluster on Embellishment and Wide Format because those are genuinely the strongest cross-sell signals in the book — a real structural finding.

## The structural finding behind the cards

Two patterns emerged while building this set, worth stating plainly because they shape where cross-sell effort should go:

**1. Embellishment and Wide Format dominate because they are the genuine next-purchase for customers who buy books and display work.** Not the method over-fitting — the real association structure. Customers who buy Soft/Hard Cover Books and Display/Installation reliably add Embellishment and Wide Format. That's the single most actionable cross-sell direction in the data.

**2. A number of high-revenue accounts are saturated — they already buy what they'd be predicted to buy — and several more already do embellishment within existing jobs.** Those are **retention plays, not cross-sell targets**. Pitching them a product they effectively already use would waste the outreach. Identifying them is as valuable as identifying the openings: it tells the team where *not* to spend effort.

## Method

Capability defined as qb-OR-classifier (qb_capability_tag else classifier capability_tags), >=2 jobs for rule basket. Each pitch is the single best genuine-zero-order-gap by confidence × lift, floored at confidence ≥25%, support ≥30, lift >1. Standalone-Embellishment pitches are suppressed when the company already applies ≥2 embellishment finishes within jobs (the validation gate that caught the finish trap). Timing is percentile-of-own-gaps (p50/p90 of each customer's own inter-order intervals) plus seasonality and email recency. Ranking dampens revenue (log10) so confidence, lift, and timing move the order — surfacing small-but-potent accounts alongside large ones.

*All figures computed from QuickBase order history and email engagement data. Every pitch verified as a genuine zero-order gap at company grain; top-15 reconciled against raw QuickBase rows.*
